Crawl Space Water Removal: What Kenner Homeowners Should Know

A flooded crawl space rarely announces itself. It tends to turn up as a damp chill underfoot, a stale smell at the vents, or a small lake discovered the day somebody lifts the hatch. At that point the batts are typically saturated and the framing overhead has sat damp for several days. We pump the space dry, remove the ruined material and get the wood dried while it is still sound.

Once the standing water is gone, the job is only halfway done. Floor joists, beams and the subfloor absorb moisture slowly and let go of it slowly, so we take moisture readings on the wood and keep dehumidifiers running until the numbers come down, usually 3 to 5 days. We also look for where the water came from, a leaking supply line, a drain, or groundwater after rain, so it does not fill back up.

What It Looks Like

  • An increase in insects and pests that like damp, dark spaces
  • Wood flooring on the first level that has begun to curl up along the seams
  • Water beading and dripping off cold pipes or metal ducts under the floor
  • Batts of insulation drooping or dangling from the underside of the floor
  • Stale, mildew type air coming up around the vents in the floor
  • White or dark fuzzy growth on joists, beams or the subfloor
The Clock

The Damage Timeline

Day one

Water Pools Below

Water collects in the low spots and soaks into the soil. Humidity in the space jumps within hours as the insulation pulls water into itself.

Hours 24 to 48

Mold Gets Started

Paper faced insulation and moist joists are where mold is able to appear first. Soaked insulation gains weight and starts to sag off the floor above.

Day 3 to 7

Moisture Moves Upstairs

Humid air from below works its way up into the rooms. You might see floorboards cupping and notice a musty odor while the framing continues to soak.

Past one week

Wood Starts Rotting

Lumber left damp this long starts breaking down. Joists and sill plates soften, fasteners rust and the damp space attracts termites and other pests.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Inspect and Make Safe

We check for electrical hazards, sagging ducts and contaminated water before anyone crawls in. Then we map how far the water reached and take moisture readings on the joists and subfloor.

2

Pump Out Water

Standing water comes out with submersible pumps and hoses, down to the footing trenches and dips where it gathers. Mud and silt left on the liner are cleared at the same time.

3

Remove Wet Materials

Soaked insulation, ruined vapor barrier and debris are bagged and carried out. Our crew then wipes down the uncovered framing and applies antimicrobial to spots with growth or a high chance of it.

4

Dry and Verify

A dehumidifier and low profile air movers stay running beneath the floor until the lumber meters at a normal level. We recheck with a meter and give you the readings and a prevention plan.

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Pests Drawn to Moisture

A wet crawl space offers the soft wood and moisture that wood destroying insects and mice prefer. Drying the space removes much of what attracts them.

Shifting Piers and Supports

When the ground stays soaked, footings may lose support and piers can tilt or sink. Sloping floors or sticking doors in the rooms above may point to this.

Rot You Cannot See

Decay tends to begin along the upper edge of the framing or where it meets the sill, out of view from the access opening. We probe the wood to find soft spots.

Water That Is Not Clean

Water under a house is often not clean. A leaking drain line or groundwater carrying soil and waste is Category 2 or 3 and needs protective gear and disinfection.
